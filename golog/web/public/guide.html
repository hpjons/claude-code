GoLog user guide

GoLog draws graphic sediment logs. You type in the beds of a measured section, and GoLog draws the log at print scale, ready to print or put in a report. It can also tell you whether the beds are ordered.

Starting and stopping

GoLog is a single program. You don't need to install it or be online. Start it, and it opens in your web browser.

WindowsDouble-click golog.exe. The first time, Windows may say it protected your PC: click More info, then Run anyway. A black window opens next to the browser. Leave it open while you work.
macOSDouble-click golog. The first time, macOS says it can't check GoLog for malicious software, because GoLog isn't from the App Store. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway (on macOS 14 and older: right-click golog and choose Open). After that a double-click is enough. A Terminal window opens next to the browser. Leave it open while you work.
LinuxRun ./golog in a terminal.

To stop GoLog, click Stop GoLog at the bottom of the Projects page, or press Ctrl+C in its window. Closing the browser tab does not stop it. Your work is saved as you go, so stopping never loses anything.

If you start GoLog while it is already running, it just opens another browser tab on the running copy.

Projects and logs

A project holds the logs from one field area or exercise. A log is one measured section. On the Projects page, type a name and click New project. Open the project, then make a log with New log.

Adding beds

Beds are entered from the base up, as you measure them in the field. Click Add the first bed, then Add bed on top for each new one. Click any bed in the drawing to select it. The Bed tab on the right then shows everything about it:

  • Thickness, in metres or centimetres (the Units menu in the top bar).
  • Lithology: up to three rock types with percentages. Click the colour square to give one bed its own colour.
  • Grain size at the base and at the top. When they differ, the bed is drawn fining or coarsening upward.
  • Base contact: sharp, erosional (drawn wavy), gradational (dashed) or unknown.
  • Structure inside the bed, such as cross-bedding, drawn repeated inside the grain-size profile.
  • Structures & fossils drawn beside the bed: click Add symbols… and search.
  • Bioturbation, palaeocurrents (directions in degrees, e.g. 45, 60), facies, the formation or age unit that starts at this bed, and notes. Some of these fields only appear when their column is turned on in the Layout tab.

Editing on the drawing

Many changes are quicker on the drawing itself:

  • Drag a bed's top edge up or down to change its thickness.
  • Drag the grain-size edge left or right. The lower half changes the grain size at the base, the upper half at the top.
  • Drag a whole bed to move it up or down the section.
  • Drag a column edge in the header to make the column wider or narrower.

Keys, when the drawing has focus: ↑ ↓ select the next bed, Alt+↑ ↓ move it, Delete deletes it (Undo brings it back) and Esc closes it.

Undo and redo work for every change: use the ↶ ↷ buttons, Ctrl+Z, and Ctrl+Shift+Z.

Columns, scale and paper

The Layout tab sets how the log is drawn and printed:

  • Columns: show or hide each one, change its title, move it left or right, and set its width in millimetres. Columns include the age and formation, height scale, lithology, grain size, structures & fossils, bioturbation, palaeocurrent, facies and notes.
  • Scale (top bar): 1:10 to 1:1000. At 1:50, one metre of section is two centimetres on paper.
  • Paper: A4, A3 or Letter. Dashed blue lines on the drawing show where printed pages end.
  • Colour or black and white. In colour, each rock has its usual colour under its pattern.
  • Grain-size scale: untick Grain sizes to leave out the names clay, silt, vf … boul above the grain-size column, Groups to leave out mud, sand and gravel, and Column title to leave out the column's name. Untick all three for an empty header.

Export and print

Export & print… in the top bar saves the log in the form you need:

PDFPages on the chosen paper, at the log's scale, with the column headers on every page. Best for printing and sending.
Print…The same pages straight to a printer. In the print window, choose the same paper size and keep the scale at 100 %.
SVG drawingThe whole log as one drawing, to finish in Inkscape or Illustrator.
PNG pictureThe whole log as a picture for reports and slides.
CSV tableThe beds as a table for Excel or LibreOffice.
CSV for SedLogThe same table in the form SedLog's Import Log from CSV file accepts.
GoLog fileEverything in the log, to back it up or give it to someone else with GoLog. They open it with Import file on a project page.

A key to the patterns, symbols and contacts used is added below the log unless you untick it. If the columns are wider than the paper, the pages are shrunk to fit and the window tells you that the scale is no longer exact.

Statistics

Statistics in the top bar first shows what the section is made of: bed thicknesses, and how much of the section each lithology, grain size, contact type and facies takes up. Then it tests whether the beds are ordered:

  • Are the facies ordered? The grid shows how often each facies (rows) is followed by each other facies (columns), counting only changes of facies. In an ordered section each facies is mostly followed by one other, and the Markov order number m is close to 1. In a random one, m is close to 0.
  • Do beds thicken or thin upward? The runs number r is high when the section has long runs of beds that get thicker or thinner upward.

Each number is compared with 5000 copies of the same beds shuffled into random order (the bars in the chart). p is the share of shuffled copies that score as high as your log or higher. A p below 0.01 is very strong evidence that the order is real and not chance. Above 0.10 there is no evidence of order. The facies test needs a facies on the beds: turn on the Facies column in the Layout tab, then give each bed a facies code, such as 1, 2, 3 or St, Sh, Fm. Save as CSV writes all the numbers for a spreadsheet.

The method is from P. M. Burgess (2016), Identifying ordered strata: evidence, methods, and meaning, Journal of Sedimentary Research 86, 148–167. SedLog's Order Analysis uses the same method and gives the same numbers in most cases.

Logs from SedLog

On a project's page, Import file… opens logs saved by SedLog (.slg), SedLog templates (.tem), CSV files, and GoLog files. You see a preview first, with a list of anything that could not be brought over exactly. For a CSV file from elsewhere, GoLog matches the columns by their headings, and you choose the rest from menus.

In the Layout tab, Use a SedLog layout… takes the columns and scale from a SedLog template.

Your data and backups

All your projects are kept in one file, golog.db, in this folder:

Windows%AppData%\GoLog (paste this into the File Explorer address bar)
macOS~/Library/Application Support/GoLog
Linux~/.local/share/GoLog

To back up everything, stop GoLog and copy golog.db somewhere safe. To move your work to another computer, copy the file into the same folder there. To share a single log, use Export & print… › GoLog file.

If something goes wrong

The browser doesn't open

Open the address shown in GoLog's window, usually http://127.0.0.1:7341.

GoLog says another program is using the address

Usually this is an earlier GoLog that was paused with Ctrl+Z rather than stopped. In that terminal, type fg and press Enter, then press Ctrl+C. Or restart the computer. GoLog still starts, but on a different address.

"Not saved – retrying" in the top bar

GoLog has stopped, or its window was closed. Start GoLog again but don't reload the page. It tries again every few seconds and says "Saved" once GoLog is back. Reloading before that would lose the changes made since the last save.

The printed log is the wrong size

Choose the same paper size in the print window as in the Layout tab, and turn off "fit to page" or "shrink to fit". Or print the PDF, which keeps the scale.
